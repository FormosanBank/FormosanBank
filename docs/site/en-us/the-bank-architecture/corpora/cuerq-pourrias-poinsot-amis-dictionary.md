# Cuerq & Pourrias (2002) Amis–Chinese Dictionary

*O Citig no Amis* (阿美語－漢語字典, also known as 博利亞潘世光阿漢字典) is a 600-page Amis–Chinese dictionary compiled by
Gérard Cuerq (顧向前, Ci Ka Ogto), with the Amis text revised by Louis Pourrias (博利亞), and published in 2002 by the
Paris Foreign Missions Society (Missions Étrangères de Paris). It is the Chinese companion to the older Amis–French
dictionary of Maurice Poinsot and Louis Pourrias. The book labels many entries and readings with the variety they belong
to: 南方話 {S}, 北方話 {N}, 鳳林話 {F}, 富田語 {T} and Zakiraya 話 {Z}.

This corpus contains the dictionary's head words and the derived forms and equivalents listed under them, each with its
Chinese definition, plus the Amis example sentences printed in the entries with their Chinese translations: 23,316
head-word entries and 4,210 examples in all. The text comes from the g0v community's crowd transcription, typed twice
(2017 and 2020) line by line against the page scans, and every entry records the printed page and line it came from. The
French translations of the first four pages, hand-checked upstream, are included alongside the Chinese.

Entries marked {Z} are Sakizaya (698) and are published as Sakizaya. The others are Amis, filed by the book's labels: {N}
and {S} as Southern, {F}, {T} and unmarked entries as Xiuguluan, and two readings labelled with places on the coast
(豐濱, Tingalaw) as Coastal.

## **Notes and Issues**

<!-- Known limitations, source defects, and caveats a user should know before using
     this corpus. Keep it user-facing (what affects the data), not process history.
     Write "None known." if there are none. -->

**Spelling.** The book uses a Church-style Amis spelling: `g` for ŋ, `o` where Southern Amis is now written `u`, and
`f` where Southern Amis now writes `v`. The standardized tier converts this to the Ortho113 standard: `g`→`ng` throughout,
and `o`→`u` and `f`→`v` in the Southern files. The compiler wrote Sakizaya in the same spelling, and the Sakizaya
standard tier is converted with `g`→`ng`, `o`→`u`, `f`→`b`, `r`→`l`.

**One entry per form.** A head word and each form listed with it (derived forms, `=` equivalents, and pairs of forms
printed with one shared definition) are separate entries with the same translation. Forms that differ by one or two
letters within one entry and dialect, with identical translations, are kept together as alternative spellings of one
entry. Roots that the book never defines on their own are not entries; they are named in the notes of the forms derived
from them.

**Dialect labels are the book's.** Where the book labels a reading with a variety, that reading appears only in that
variety's file. Words with no label of their own inherit the label of the head word they are listed under.

**Translations in other languages.** Scientific names printed in the entries are kept as Latin translations, and English,
French and German names as translations in those languages, each marked in its notes. The compiler's "(?)" is kept as the
note "translation uncertain".

**Known gaps.** 54 printed lines were marked blank by mistake in the 2020 transcription and are not yet in the corpus. Ten
entries that could not be read with confidence were left out. About 25 entries mix a dialect label with several Amis forms
in a way the book does not make unambiguous, and some Amis phrases quoted inside Chinese definitions remain in notes. All
of these are listed in the corpus README for future work.
