# One Hundred Paiwan Texts

One hundred morphologically analyzed Paiwan texts from Early and Whitehorn (2003).

## Languages and dialects

Every file is Paiwan, `xml:lang="pwn"`. The `dialect` attribute on each `TEXT`
records one of four Paiwan dialects, or `unknown`:

| `dialect` | files | texts (`PaiwanCh2_NNN.xml`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Southern` | 41 | 001–041 |
| `Central` | 17 | 042–051, 058–062, 095, 098 |
| `Eastern` | 6 | 052–057 |
| `Northern` | 32 | 063–089, 092–094, 096, 100 |
| `unknown` | 4 | 090, 091, 097, 099 |

The dialect values are not in the Word document the corpus is built from; the
build takes them from the previously published corpus (see "What the rebuild
needs besides the source" below), and writes `unknown` where that record has
none.

#### Dialect coverage

The source does not identify a supported dialect for texts `090`, `091`, `097`, and `099`. Their dialect remains `unknown`, and their generated phonology uses the default Paiwan profile. These tiers should be revisited if source-supported dialect metadata becomes available.

## Source

The source is Early, R. J., and Whitehorn, J. (2003), *One Hundred Paiwan
Texts*, published by Pacific Linguistics. Each sentence is printed as three
interlinear lines (the natural Paiwan text, its morpheme analysis, and a gloss
line) followed by a free English translation. All 100 texts are published
here. R. J. Early supplied the two Word documents the corpus is built from (see
"License"). There is no audio.

Users are encouraged to consult the published source. A PDF is available from the [Australian National University repository](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/5f377c02-9051-40b3-b839-7108229b3f84/content), which also provides the grammatical and cultural context for the texts.

#### Overview

The 100 Paiwan Texts corpus contains 100 morphologically analyzed and glossed Paiwan texts from Early and Whitehorn (2003). The published XML has 2,921 sentence elements, including 2,916 source sentences and 5 complete variants for optional source tokens.

#### Contents

- 100 XML files and TEXT elements
- 2,916 source sentences plus 5 complete omitted-token variants
- 2,921 S elements
- 24,556 W elements
- 36,938 M elements
- 64,515 globally unique TEXT, S, W, and M IDs
- all 64,198 IDs from the previous public version preserved

The S tier preserves each natural Paiwan line. The W and M tiers preserve the separate source analysis and gloss lines. Standard FORM and PHON tiers are generated by the pinned FormosanBank pipeline.

## Notes and caveats

Each item is described in full in the section named in brackets (those
sections are under "What each step does" below).

- **One morpheme has no gloss.** `078S4W19M0c`, the final `-i` of
  `t<al>e-talem-i` in `078S4`, is published with no `TRANSL` because the
  source prints four form units but only three gloss units. The three
  validator findings this causes (`G001` HARD, `G002` and `V064` SOFT) are
  accepted. ["The one unglossed morpheme"]
- **The source's `?` glosses are published as `<UNCLEAR/>`**: 356 whole
  glosses (338 `M`, 18 `W`) and the `?` pieces of 291 `W` glosses. A `?` next
  to an infix marker (24 `W` glosses) or attached to a word (4 glosses) is kept
  as written, and two sentence translations keep a `?` standing for an
  untranslated word. ["The source's `?` gloss is published as `<UNCLEAR/>`"]
- **Sixteen sentences are corrected by a reviewed decision table**
  (`CodeAndDocs/standard_surface_decisions.tsv`): 11 have the source's
  `(?)` uncertain-reading annotation removed from the original FORM and
  recorded in `notes`, and 5 with a parenthesised optional token are published
  as two complete sentences. ["How sentence-level corrections are recorded"]
- **Hyphens differ between tiers.** The original tier keeps the source's
  hyphens (153 sentences); the standard tier has none. ["Hyphens are not part
  of the standard orthography"]
- **Quotation marks.** The cleaner rewrote 22 `'` as `"` in the original tier
  and left 27 flagged `'` alone because they might be the glottal letter; one
  of those, `008S28`, is corrected by a recorded hand edit, and so is `050S39`,
  whose two `'` the cleaner no longer rewrites or flags. ["Validation",
  "Recorded hand edits"]
- **Four texts have no dialect.** Texts `090`, `091`, `097` and `099` are
  `dialect="unknown"`. ["Dialect coverage", under "Languages and dialects"]

#### The one unglossed morpheme, and the three findings it causes

The source's analysis of sentence `078S4`, word 19 (`t<al>e-talem-i`) prints
four form units but only three gloss units (`al=te-talem-i` against
`qal=do-plant`), leaving the final `-i` without a gloss.

Morpheme `078S4W19M0c` is therefore published with **no `TRANSL` element at
all**. An absent gloss is the honest record of an absent source gloss. It is
deliberately not filled with a placeholder: this source writes `?` itself for
morphemes its authors could not identify (published as `<UNCLEAR/>`, see the
next section), so a marker supplied by FormosanBank would be indistinguishable
from the authors' own. The
`notes` attribute on the word's `TRANSL` records that the gap is intentional.

Three validator findings follow directly from that decision, and are accepted:

| Rule | Severity | What it reports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `V064` `every_M_has_TRANSL` | Soft | `078S4W19M0c` has no gloss |
| `G002` `M_count_matches_gloss_units` | Soft | the word has 4 morphemes but its gloss implies 3 |
| `G001` `marker_skeleton_parity` | **Hard** | `t<al>e-talem-i` and `do<qal>-plant` differ by that one unit |

There is no way to publish an unglossed morpheme without them, and the
alternative — inventing a gloss — is worse. The build does not simply ignore
hard findings: `review_qc_findings.py` pins all three to this exact word and
morpheme and fails if a fourth appears, if one moves, or if any other morpheme
in the corpus loses its gloss.

#### The source's `?` gloss is published as `<UNCLEAR/>`

Where the analyst could not identify a morpheme, the source glosses it with a
bare `?` (for example `001S7`, morpheme `ta`). FormosanBank has an element for
exactly that, so a word or morpheme gloss whose whole text is `?`, `??` or
`???` is published as `<UNCLEAR/>`, with the `TRANSL`'s attributes unchanged:

```xml
<!-- before -->  <TRANSL xml:lang="eng" kindOf="original">?</TRANSL>
<!-- after  -->  <TRANSL xml:lang="eng" kindOf="original"><UNCLEAR/></TRANSL>
```

This applies to 356 glosses: 338 morpheme glosses and 18 word glosses. A `?`
that is one piece of a longer word gloss is converted too, in the 291 word
glosses listed in `CodeAndDocs/unclear_gloss_placeholders.tsv` (104 distinct
glosses): `?-what` becomes `<UNCLEAR/>-what`. A `?` next to an infix marker
(24 word glosses, e.g. `?<af>-complete`) or attached to a word (4 glosses,
e.g. `decide?`) is kept as written. Sentence translations are not changed; two
of them contain a `?` standing for an untranslated word (`Well if you had ?, …`).
No `FORM` is `<UNCLEAR/>`.

#### What the rebuild needs besides the source

Two things cannot be derived from the Word document, so the rebuild reconciles
against the corpus as it was last published:

* **Morpheme ids** — 36,815 come straight from the published corpus, and 123
  more are derived from them by suffixing when one published `M` splits into
  several source units (`M0` → `M0a`, `M0b`, `M0c`). Published ids must stay
  stable, so they cannot simply be renumbered. Sentence and word ids *are*
  computed from the source and only checked against the published set.
* **Four `TEXT` attributes** — `id`, `citation`, `BibTeX_citation` and
  `dialect`.

That skeleton is committed as data —
[`baseline_text_metadata.tsv`](CodeAndDocs/data/baseline_text_metadata.tsv)
and
[`baseline_morphemes.tsv`](CodeAndDocs/data/baseline_morphemes.tsv)
— rather than read from a git commit, so the corpus rebuilds from a checkout of
those files alone, with no dependency on history remaining reachable.
`export_baseline.py` regenerates them from an XML tree so they stay auditable
against the corpus they came from.

The build otherwise pins nothing: shared tooling runs from the live checkout,
on the bank's model that tooling improves and corpora are regenerated against
it, so a rebuild picks up later improvements and any resulting XML diff should
be reviewed rather than suppressed.

#### How sentence-level corrections are recorded (summary)

A handful of sentences need a correction the blanket pipeline cannot make on its
own. The corpus uses **both** recording mechanisms, for the two different kinds
of correction — and **neither edits a generated tier**. Both act on the original
`FORM` before `standardize.py` and `add_phonology.py` run, so the standard and
phonology tiers are always derived, never repaired afterwards.

**A systematic, source-derived rule** goes in a reviewed decision table,
[`standard_surface_decisions.tsv`](CodeAndDocs/standard_surface_decisions.tsv),
which `apply_manual_corrections.py` and `rebuild_xml.py` apply and which
`normalize_sentence_standards.py` checks after `standardize.py` runs. Every
decision is listed there in full, so readers can check each one against the
source. All 16 rows concern parenthesised material:

* **11 record a source reading marked uncertain.** Where the source appends a
  bracketed query to a word on its plain-text line — `paqeteleng(?)` — that is
  an editorial annotation *about* the text, not part of it.
  `apply_manual_corrections.py` removes it from the sentence's original `FORM`
  **before `standardize.py` runs** and records what it removed in that FORM's
  `notes` attribute. `?` is the glottal letter in Ferrell's orthography, so a
  `(?)` left in place standardizes to a glottal stop the language does not
  have — the corpus previously repaired the standard `FORM` afterwards but not
  the standard `PHON`, which still carried the invented consonant.
* **5 publish a parenthesised optional token as two complete sentences.** Where
  the source prints `kemljang (aken) tu …`, both complete readings are published
  as separate `S` elements rather than leaving a parenthesis inside a sentence.

The table is preferred to a recorded diff for these because each row carries the
source form, what the blanket pipeline produced, the corrected form, the
decision and its evidence. The build re-derives the change every time. Every
correction is witness-gated — the sentence's original form must still match the
reviewed source, or the build fails rather than correcting drifted text — and
the pass is idempotent.

**A genuine one-off editorial judgement** goes in
[`manual_edits.xml`](CodeAndDocs/manual_edits.xml),
the standard FormosanBank mechanism, replayed by `apply_manual_edits.py` and
summarised in
[`manual_edits.md`](CodeAndDocs/manual_edits.md).
There are exactly two such records, `008S28` and `050S39`. In `008S28` the source prints its inner
quotation with curly single quotes; the cleaner folds those to `'` and then
declines to rewrite them as `"`, because the translation does not place the
quotation confidently enough to rule out a glottal reading — `'` is the glottal
letter in this orthography, so the cleaner logs the case rather than guessing.
Here it is a quotation, so the edit sets `"aa"` in the original tier. The build
fails if either record ever becomes a no-op, since a no-op would mean the pipeline
now produces the content by itself and the record is obsolete.

#### Hyphens are not part of the standard orthography (summary)

Ferrell's §1.6 gives three interlinear levels. The plain-text first line — the
one that becomes the sentence `FORM` — legitimately contains hyphens, while the
second line carries the morpheme analysis. The **original** tier keeps every one
of them: 153 sentences contain hyphens, and the `G010` warnings report exactly
those.

The **standard** tier carries none. A hyphen is a segmentation marker rather
than a letter of the standard orthography, so the pipeline strips it and the
decision table does not put it back. `V133` (`dash_in_S_standard_FORM`) does not
fire at all.

## Reproduction and processing

### How to reproduce

The two source files are committed alongside the build scripts, so no external input is required:

- `CodeAndDocs/Paiwan Ch2.docx` — the author-provided original
- `CodeAndDocs/Paiwan Ch2 Preprocessed.docx` — the author-provided preprocessed text, the file the generator parses

Both are verified against `CodeAndDocs/data/source_checksums.sha256` before the build starts. Set `PAIWAN_SOURCE_ROOT` only to point the build at copies held elsewhere; they must match the same checksums.

Install the two pinned Python dependencies and run the clean-room check from the repository root:

```bash
python3 -m pip install -r Corpora/HundredPaiwanStories/CodeAndDocs/requirements.txt
Corpora/HundredPaiwanStories/CodeAndDocs/generate_xml.sh --check
```

The script verifies the source checksums in `CodeAndDocs/data/source_checksums.sha256`, rebuilds the XML against the current FormosanBank checkout, runs the full validator and test suite, and compares the result with the published files in `XML/Paiwan/`. `--check` fails if the XML, `reports/rebuild/` or `manual_edits.md` differ. The four QC logs in `reports/qc/` (`qc-summary.md`, `soft_findings_review.json`, `cleaner_warnings.csv`, `quote_corrections.csv`) describe what the shared cleaner and validators report, so they change whenever those tools do; `--check` prints any difference in them as a note with the diff instead of failing. Use `--write` only when intentionally regenerating the corpus; it also refreshes those logs.

`CodeAndDocs/requirements.txt` pins `lxml`, `python-docx` and `regex`. With
no argument the script runs in `--write` mode. Three environment variables
change where it looks: `PAIWAN_SOURCE_ROOT` (the folder holding the two Word
files; default `CodeAndDocs/`), `PAIWAN_PYTHON` (the Python interpreter;
default `python3`) and `PAIWAN_FORMOSANBANK_ROOT` (the FormosanBank checkout
whose shared tools are run; default the checkout this corpus sits in). The
build works in a temporary folder, `/tmp/hundred-paiwan-rebuild.XXXXXX`,
deleted on exit, and writes into the corpus folder only in `--write` mode.

**POL-047 deviation:** the validators run *inside* `generate_xml.sh` rather
than in a separate `validate.sh`, and that is deliberate here. This build's
`reports/qc/*` are published corpus artifacts, and
`scripts/review_qc_findings.py` gates the build on the validators' findings —
it pins the one by-design `G001` finding to its exact location and rejects any
other HARD finding. Splitting QC out would install XML before the gate had run, weakening a
safety property this corpus is built around. The cost POL-047 warns about is
accepted knowingly: a validator failure here blocks the rebuild, and
investigating one means running the validators by hand against the build tree.

[`CodeAndDocs/provenance.json`](CodeAndDocs/provenance.json) records which FormosanBank commit the published output was built against (POL-052) — documentation only; rebuilds run with the tools in whatever checkout invokes them.

### What each step does

The corpus was rebuilt in August 2026 from two checksum-pinned Word documents supplied by the author. All 268 rendered source pages were reviewed. Both Word documents are published with the corpus under `CodeAndDocs/`, so the XML rebuilds from a FormosanBank checkout alone; only the private correspondence recording the author's permission is withheld.

The rebuild preserves the natural Paiwan line at the sentence tier and the separate source analysis and gloss lines at the word and morpheme tiers. It also:

* restores the first sentence of story 091, nine omitted final words, and four XML files missing from the earlier development output;
* expands five optional tokens into complete sentence variants;
* records two word-alignment decisions, three translation-note extractions, one punctuation repair, four text-metadata corrections, and 16 exact sentence-surface decisions;
* preserves all 64,198 identifiers from the previous public version while assigning unique identifiers to restored material; and
* leaves the one source morpheme without a gloss, final `-i` in sentence `078S4`, genuinely unglossed rather than inventing a meaning for it.

The deterministic pipeline rebuilds the original tiers, runs the shared cleaner, creates standard forms, generates phonology, resolves the source orthography's question-mark and glottal-stop ambiguity, applies the reviewed sentence decisions, and runs structural, text, gloss, and source-scrape validation. One row was removed from the shared Ferrell conversion table: `Ḍ → dr`, which mapped the uppercase retroflex to a lowercase output. The row was not redundant — it overrode the standardizer's case-variant derivation, so proper names lost their capital in the standard tier (`Ḍiququ → driququ`). Without it the derivation produces case-preserving `Ḍ → Dr`, giving `Driququ`. The change affects standard-tier capitalization only: it leaves every one of the corpus's 128,830 phonology elements byte-identical, because `add_phonology.py` matches orthography letters case-insensitively and already read `Ḍ` as `ɖ`. The corrected table passes its standalone audit.

The final audit reports one hard finding, accepted and documented below, and no others across the four validator families. The remaining soft findings are fully classified as source notation or canonical infix and reduplication structures. The [corpus README](#reproduction-and-processing) documents the reproducible workflow, and the [audit report](CodeAndDocs/reports/audit-report.md) records the evidence.

#### Source review

The rebuild was checked against all 268 rendered pages of the two checksum-pinned Word documents. It restores story 091's omitted first sentence, nine omitted final words, five complete optional-token variants, and four missing legacy XML files. It also records two word-alignment decisions, three translation-note extractions, one punctuation repair, four TEXT metadata corrections, and 16 exact sentence-level surface decisions.

#### The steps, in the order `generate_xml.sh` runs them

1. **Dependency check.** Imports `docx`, `lxml` and `regex`; if any is
   missing, it stops and prints the `pip install` command.
2. **Source checksums.** For each line of `data/source_checksums.sha256`, the
   named file under `PAIWAN_SOURCE_ROOT` must exist and its SHA-256 must
   match, or the build stops.
3. **Conversion-table check.** Runs the shared
   `QC/validation/validate_conversion_table.py` on
   `Orthographies/Ferrell/Paiwan.tsv`, `Orthographies/Ortho113/Paiwan.tsv` and
   `Orthographies/ConversionTables/Paiwan_Ferrell_113.tsv`.
4. **`scripts/rebuild_xml.py`** (`--source "Paiwan Ch2 Preprocessed.docx"
   --output <build>/XML --reports <build>/reports/rebuild`) builds the original
   tiers. `scripts/paiwan_source.py` parses the Word document by paragraph
   style: a `Heading 4` paragraph starts a story; an `InterlineText` paragraph
   is the natural Paiwan line (its first tab cell is the printed sentence
   number); the next paragraph is the morpheme line, the one after it the
   gloss line, and translation-style paragraphs after that are the free
   translation; comment-style paragraphs become notes. Four legacy-font code
   points are mapped (`î` → `ɫ`, `ï` → `Ɫ`, `÷` → `ḍ`, `ª` → `Ḍ`) and the text
   is NFC-normalized. The parser requires exactly stories 001–100 and 2,916
   sentences. For each story the script writes one `PaiwanCh2_NNN.xml`:
   - `TEXT` gets `xml:lang="pwn"`, `copyright="CC BY-NC 4.0"`, `id`,
     `citation`, `BibTeX_citation` and `dialect` from
     `data/baseline_text_metadata.tsv` (`unknown` where the dialect cell is
     empty), and `source` from the story heading in the Word document, which
     must equal the published value unless `data/text_metadata_corrections.tsv`
     (4 rows: stories 081, 091, 092, 098) records a reviewed correction.
   - Each `S` gets the natural line as its original `FORM` and the free
     translation as an English `TRANSL` (source comments go in its `notes`).
     Sentence ids are `NNNS<sentence number>`, except the 30 rows of
     `data/sentence_id_exceptions.tsv` (a repeated printed number in story 050,
     and story 091, whose omitted first sentence becomes `091S0` so the
     published `091S1`–`091S28` keep their ids).
   - Each `W` gets the natural word as its original `FORM` and the source gloss
     as `TRANSL kindOf="original"`; each `M` gets one analysis unit and its
     gloss. Infix positions inside a root are taken from the published corpus
     where it has them and otherwise inferred by aligning against the natural
     word; inferred ones are listed in `reports/rebuild/gap_inference.tsv`.
   - Reviewed data tables, each with a fixed row count the script checks:
     `data/optional_variants.tsv` (5: the source's `(token)` is written without
     its parentheses, and a second complete `S` without the token is added as
     `<id>-omit`), `data/word_alignment_overrides.tsv` (2: `009S2` word 7,
     whose analysis spans two cells, and `078S4` word 19, the unglossed `-i`),
     `data/translation_note_extractions.tsv` (3: a labelled note at the end of
     a translation moves to its `notes` attribute),
     `data/translation_punctuation_corrections.tsv` (1: `057S3`'s unmatched
     parenthesis is closed) and `data/recovered_final_words.tsv` (9: final words
     the earlier version lost, each checked against the source).
   - Morpheme ids come from `data/baseline_morphemes.tsv`, as described next.
   The script stops if any published id disappears, if the totals are not
   64,198 published and 64,515 rebuilt ids, or if any morpheme other than
   `078S4W19M0c` lacks a gloss. It writes `reports/rebuild/id_reconciliation.tsv`
   (every added or split id) and `reports/rebuild/rebuild_summary.json`.

   The build deliberately does not pin shared tooling — the bank's model is that tooling improves and corpora are regenerated against it — so a rebuild picks up later improvements, and a resulting XML diff should be reviewed rather than suppressed.

   The rebuild does reconcile against the corpus **as it was last published**, because two things cannot be derived from the source document:

   - **Morpheme ids.** 36,815 come straight from the published corpus and 123 more are derived from them by suffixing when one published `M` splits into several source units (`M0` → `M0a`, `M0b`, `M0c`). POL-037 requires published ids to be stable, so they cannot simply be renumbered. Sentence and word ids *are* computed from the source and only checked against the published set.
   - **Four `TEXT` attributes** — `id`, `citation`, `BibTeX_citation` and `dialect` — which the Word document does not contain.

   That skeleton is **committed as data**, not read from a git commit:

   - [`CodeAndDocs/data/baseline_text_metadata.tsv`](CodeAndDocs/data/baseline_text_metadata.tsv) — one row per file, the `TEXT` attributes.
   - [`CodeAndDocs/data/baseline_morphemes.tsv`](CodeAndDocs/data/baseline_morphemes.tsv) — one row per published morpheme, in document order: its sentence, word, id and original `FORM`.

   Together they are exactly what the reconciliation reads and nothing more, so the corpus rebuilds from a checkout of these files alone — no git history required, and nothing breaks if an old commit becomes unreachable. `CodeAndDocs/scripts/export_baseline.py` regenerates the tables from an XML tree, so they can be re-derived and audited against the corpus they came from; it is not part of the build.
5. **Shared cleaner**, `QC/cleaning/clean_xml.py --corpora_path XML`. Its two
   logs are moved to `reports/qc/cleaner_warnings.csv` and
   `reports/qc/quote_corrections.csv` (see "Validation" below).
6. **Recorded hand edits**, `QC/cleaning/apply_manual_edits.py --corpora_path
   XML`, with `CodeAndDocs/manual_edits.xml` copied into the build tree. The
   build stops unless the output contains `apply: 2 edit(s) across 2 file(s);
   0 no-op(s)`. It writes `manual_edits.md`. See "Recorded hand edits" below.
7. **`scripts/apply_manual_corrections.py --corpora-path XML`** removes the
   source's `(?)` from the 11 sentences whose decision in
   `standard_surface_decisions.tsv` is `remove_parenthesized_uncertainty`, and
   records it in the original `FORM`'s `notes`. The build stops unless it
   prints `reviewed_corrections=11` and `corrected_originals=11`. See "How
   sentence-level corrections are recorded" below.
8. **Standard tier and phonology.** `QC/utilities/standardize.py --corpora_path
   XML --language Paiwan --tsv_path
   Orthographies/ConversionTables/Paiwan_Ferrell_113.tsv` writes the standard
   `FORM`s; `QC/utilities/add_phonology.py --corpora_path XML --orthography
   Ferrell` writes the `PHON`s. Standardization uses the shared `Orthographies/ConversionTables/Paiwan_Ferrell_113.tsv`. The corpus previously carried a corrected private copy; that correction (see "Source review" above) now lives in the shared table, so a rebuild requires a FormosanBank checkout that includes it. See "Hyphens are not part of the standard orthography" below.
9. **`fix_ferrell.py --corpora-path XML`** (its docstring: "Resolve Ferrell
   question-mark and glottal-stop ambiguity"). In every `FORM`, a `'` that
   follows a letter at the end of the text, or that stands before a closing
   `"` at the end of the text or right after a letter, is written back as
   `?`; in every `PHON`, a `ʔ` in the same positions is deleted.
10. **`normalize_sentence_standards.py --corpora-path XML`**, run twice. It
    checks all 16 rows of `standard_surface_decisions.tsv` against the XML
    (the original `FORM` must match the reviewed source, the standard `FORM`
    must equal the reviewed result, each of the 5 optional-token sentences
    must have its complete `-omit` sentence, no standard `PHON` may carry
    `- = < > ~ ( )`). Both runs must print `exact_decisions=16`,
    `corrected_forms=0`, `corrected_phon=0` and
    `normalized_complete_variants=0`: it changes nothing, and running it twice
    proves the result is stable.
11. **`scripts/apply_unclear_glosses.py --corpora-path XML`** writes the
    source's `?` glosses as `<UNCLEAR/>`. The build stops unless it prints
    `unclear_w_glosses=18`, `unclear_m_glosses=338`,
    `placeholder_w_glosses=291`, `placeholder_m_glosses=0` and
    `omitted_sentence_translations=0`. See "The source's `?` gloss is
    published as `<UNCLEAR/>`" below.
12. **Validators.** A folder of links to every other corpus in the checkout is
    made so `validate_xml.py` can check that no `TEXT` id collides with
    another corpus. Then, each writing a CSV under `reports/qc/` in the build
    tree (not committed): `QC/validation/validate_xml.py by_path --path XML
    --published-corpora published-corpora`, `validate_text.py by_path`,

    normalized to LF.
13. **QC gate**, `scripts/review_qc_findings.py`, reads the four CSVs, the XML
    and `standard_surface_decisions.tsv`, and writes

    See "What the QC gate fails on" below.
14. **Corpus tests.** `python -m unittest discover -s CodeAndDocs/tests`

    language-named folder), and the rebuild reports, the four QC logs and

#### What the QC gate fails on

The gate is built so that changes to the shared
validators do not break this corpus's rebuild:

- **Any HARD finding** other than the accepted `G001` on `078S4W19` fails,
  whatever rule it comes from.
- **Reviewed rules** (`V061`, `V064`, `V122`, `G001`, `G002`, `G010`; `G003`
  must report nothing): every finding must be explained against the XML (for
  example, each `V061` must be a reduplicated word), and a count above the
  reviewed one fails. A count below it is reported in `qc-summary.md`, not
  failed: the gate's XML-derived checks (element inventory, parentheses,
  hyphen-free standard tier, the unglossed morpheme) still hold the data
  itself, so a lower count means the validator now reports less.
- **Any other rule** is unreviewed: its SOFT and WARN findings are listed in
  `qc-summary.md` under "Findings from rules this corpus has not reviewed",
  printed as a `NOTE:` during the build, and do not stop it. At the last
  rebuild (2026-10-18) these were `V135` (66), `V153` (43) and `G014` (5).

#### Recorded hand edits

Two corrections in this corpus are one-off editorial judgements rather than
a rule, so they use the standard FormosanBank mechanism:
[`CodeAndDocs/manual_edits.xml`](CodeAndDocs/manual_edits.xml), replayed by
`QC/cleaning/apply_manual_edits.py` and summarised for readers in
[`CodeAndDocs/manual_edits.md`](CodeAndDocs/manual_edits.md).

The first record is `008S28`. The source prints its inner quotation with curly
single quotes (`‘aa’`); the cleaner folds those to `'` and then declines to
rewrite them as `"`, because the English translation does not place the
quotation confidently enough to rule out a glottal reading — `'` is the glottal
letter in this orthography, so the cleaner is deliberately conservative and logs
the case (rule `c030`) instead of guessing. Here it is a quotation, so the edit
sets `"aa"` in the original FORM at the sentence, word and morpheme tiers.

The second record is `050S39`. The source prints `“sa-u; ‘idu,’ aya-u, kina.”`
(“Go and tell him to come here, mother.”): `‘idu,’` is a quotation inside the
quotation. The cleaner folds the curly marks to `'` and leaves them, so without
the edit the standard tier would read them as glottal stops (standard PHON
`sau ʔiduʔ ajau kina`). The edit sets `"idu,"` in the sentence's original FORM
(the word and morpheme FORMs, `id-u`, carry no quotation mark), giving
standard PHON `sau idu ajau kina`.

The step runs after the cleaner and *before* `standardize.py` and
`add_phonology.py`, because an edit record carries no standard FORM and no PHON
— both are regenerated from the edited original. The build asserts exactly two
edits apply and **fails on a no-op**: if the pipeline ever produces this
content by itself the record is obsolete (POL-030) and should be pruned, not
left to pass silently.

#### How sentence-level corrections are recorded

A handful of sentences need a correction that the blanket pipeline cannot make
on its own. This corpus uses **both** recording mechanisms, for the two
different kinds of correction — and **neither edits a generated tier**. Both act
on the original FORM before `standardize.py` and `add_phonology.py` run, so the
standard and phonology tiers are always derived, never repaired afterwards.

**A systematic, source-derived rule** goes in a reviewed decision table,
[`CodeAndDocs/standard_surface_decisions.tsv`](CodeAndDocs/standard_surface_decisions.tsv).
Every row is listed there in full, so you can check each decision yourself; the
table has 16 rows, all of them about parenthesised material:

- **11 record a source reading marked uncertain.** Where the source appends a
  bracketed query to a word on its plain-text line — `paqeteleng(?)` — that is
  an editorial annotation *about* the text, not part of it.
  `CodeAndDocs/scripts/apply_manual_corrections.py` removes it from the
  sentence's original FORM before `standardize.py` runs and records what it
  removed in that FORM's `notes` attribute.

  This matters beyond tidiness: `?` is the **glottal letter** in Ferrell's
  orthography, so a `(?)` left in the original standardizes to a glottal stop
  the language does not have. The corpus previously repaired the standard
  *FORM* after the fact but not the standard *PHON*, which still carried the
  invented consonant (`qəpuqəpuiʔ`). Correcting the original fixes both tiers
  at once.
- **5 publish a parenthesised optional token as two complete sentences.** Where
  the source prints `kemljang (aken) tu ...`, the corpus publishes both complete
  readings as separate `S` elements rather than leaving a parenthesis in a
  sentence, with the omitted-token variant recorded in
  `alternate_standard_form`. These are resolved when the XML is generated.

Each row carries the source form, what the blanket pipeline produced
(`legacy_blanket_form`), the corrected form, the decision, and the evidence for
it — so a reader can audit the change against the source, and the build
re-derives it every time rather than replaying a recorded diff. Every
correction is witness-gated: the sentence's original FORM must still match the
reviewed source or the build fails rather than correcting drifted text, and the
pass is idempotent. `normalize_sentence_standards.py` now changes nothing — it
runs after `standardize.py` purely to verify that all 16 decisions hold.

**A one-off editorial judgement** goes in `manual_edits.xml` — see
"Recorded hand edits" above.

#### Hyphens are not part of the standard orthography

Ferrell's §1.6 gives three interlinear levels, and the plain-text first line —
the one that becomes the `S` FORM — legitimately contains hyphens where the
second line carries the morpheme analysis. The **original** tier keeps every one
of those hyphens: 153 sentences contain them, and `G010` reports exactly those.

The **standard** tier carries none. A hyphen is a segmentation marker rather
than a letter of the standard orthography, so the blanket pipeline strips it and
the decision table does not put it back. `V133` (`dash_in_S_standard_FORM`)
therefore does not fire at all, and `normalize_sentence_standards.py` rejects any
decision row that would reintroduce a hyphen.

The port review also removed one row from the shared Ferrell conversion table:
`Ḍ → dr`, which mapped the uppercase retroflex to a lowercase output. The row
was not redundant — it took precedence over the case-variant derivation in
`QC/utilities/_case_variants.py`, so proper names lost their capital in the
standard tier (`Ḍiququ → driququ`, `Ḍaḍengeraw → dradrengeraw`). Without it the
standardizer derives the case-preserving `Ḍ → Dr` from the lowercase `ḍ → dr`
rule, and those names standardize as `Driququ` and `Dradrengeraw`. Uppercase `Ḍ`
occurs in no other corpus today, so removing the row changed nothing outside
these texts, and the fix now lives in the shared table for every Paiwan corpus.
It is phonology-neutral: all 128,830 `PHON` elements are unchanged, because
`add_phonology.py` matches orthography letters case-insensitively and already
read `Ḍ` as `ɖ`.

#### The one unglossed morpheme

Sentence `078S4`, word 19 (`t<al>e-talem-i`) is the single place where the
source's analysis runs short: it prints four form units but only three gloss
units (`al=te-talem-i` against `qal=do-plant`), leaving the final `-i`
unglossed.

The morpheme `078S4W19M0c` is therefore published **with no `TRANSL` at all**.
An absent gloss is the honest record of an absent source gloss. It is
deliberately not filled with a `?`: this source writes `?` itself for
morphemes it cannot identify (published as `<UNCLEAR/>`, see the next
section), and a marker of our own would be indistinguishable from the
author's. The `notes` attribute on the word's `TRANSL` records that the gap is
intentional.

#### The source's `?` gloss is published as `<UNCLEAR/>` (details)

Where the analyst could not identify a morpheme, the source glosses it with a
bare `?` (for example `001S7`, morpheme `ta`). FormosanBank has an element for
exactly that, so a `W` or `M` gloss whose whole text is `?`, `??` or `???`
(ASCII or full-width) is published as `<UNCLEAR/>`, with the `TRANSL`'s
attributes unchanged.

This applies to 356 glosses: 338 `M` and 18 `W`. Maintainer's rulings,
2026-10-17: "B. A, plus ? used as a placeholder inside a gloss", where A =
"Convert glosses that are exactly ? or ??"; "B. Glosses UNCLEAR; sentence
translations omitted"; and "if the S FORM is simply <UNCLEAR/>, no Ws. If the
W FORM is <UNCLEAR />, no Ms under it." In this corpus no sentence translation
is only a `?` (so none is omitted), and no `FORM` is `<UNCLEAR/>` (the build
fails if one ever is).

A `?` that is one piece of a longer gloss is converted too, gloss by gloss:
[`CodeAndDocs/unclear_gloss_placeholders.tsv`](CodeAndDocs/unclear_gloss_placeholders.tsv)
(columns `gloss`, `lang`) lists the glosses to convert, and in each listed
gloss every `?` bounded by `-`, `=`, `.`, whitespace or the ends of the string
becomes `<UNCLEAR/>`:

| source gloss | published |
| --- | --- |
| `?-what` | `<UNCLEAR/>-what` |
| `stat-exact-?` | `stat-exact-<UNCLEAR/>` |
| `if-main-?-outsider-nom` | `if-main-<UNCLEAR/>-outsider-nom` |

The table lists 104 distinct glosses, all `eng`, covering all 291 `W` glosses
of this kind (maintainer's ruling, 2026-10-17: "All rows in this corpus:
placeholder → UNCLEAR"). A table row that matches no gloss fails the build.

Two kinds of `?` are still published as written, because the `?` is not
bounded by one of those delimiters: a `?` next to an infix marker (24 `W`
glosses, e.g. `?<af>-complete`, `?<perf>-loc-that-still`) and a `?` attached to
a word (4 glosses: `decide?` on two `M`, `decide?<perf>` and
`past-decide?<af>` on `W`). Sentence translations are not glosses and are not
touched; two of them contain a `?` standing for an untranslated word
(`Well if you had ?, …`).

The conversion is `CodeAndDocs/scripts/apply_unclear_glosses.py`, the last
step of `generate_xml.sh` before the validators. It runs last because the
corpus's own scripts re-indent the XML with `ET.indent`, which would put
whitespace inside a `TRANSL` that holds only `<UNCLEAR/>`.

Three validator findings follow directly from that choice, and are **accepted**:

| rule | severity | what it reports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `V064` `every_M_has_TRANSL` | SOFT | `078S4W19M0c` has no gloss |
| `G002` `M_count_matches_gloss_units` | SOFT | the W has 4 `M` but its gloss implies 3 |
| `G001` `marker_skeleton_parity` | **HARD** | `t<al>e-talem-i` and `do<qal>-plant` differ by that unit |

There is no way to publish an unglossed morpheme without them; the alternative
is to invent a gloss, which is worse. `generate_xml.sh` therefore does not pass
`--exit-on-hard` to the gloss-scrape audit. It is not ungated:
`scripts/review_qc_findings.py` pins all three findings to this exact word and
morpheme and fails the build if a fourth appears, if one moves, or if any other
morpheme loses its gloss.
